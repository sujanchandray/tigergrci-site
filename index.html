The problem

Compliance stalls when the law stays stuck in PDFs.

Organizations in Bangladesh must honour national cyber law, Bangladesh Bank guidance, and international standards at once. Most tools treat local law as an afterthought. People guess what applies, what a breach means, and how to prove the work.

Unclear duties

Raw legal text does not tell you which clauses bind your organisation today.

Policies drift from controls

Written policies and the controls auditors expect stop matching. The trail goes cold.

Frameworks live apart

National law, bank packs, and global catalogues sit in separate folders with no shared path.

Automation without ownership

Suggestions that auto-pass create risk. Someone accountable must still accept each call.

How we solve it

Treat compliance as an operating problem, not a document pile. Decide what applies. Bind policy to control. Prove the fix with evidence you can defend.

How it works

Governance, risk, and compliance in plain view.

Three letters. One product story you can show a board member without a glossary.

G · Governance

The G that actually governs

A policy that nobody follows is paperwork. TigerGRCi covers drafting, review, approval, attestations, and exceptions. Every policy can connect to the controls that enforce it.

Bangladesh cyber law is handled clause by clause in the Legal Center. You decide what applies with dual approval: one person proposes, another confirms.

R · Risk

Risk you can track, not only colour in

Heat maps alone rarely satisfy a board. TigerGRCi keeps a company-wide risk register and domain workspaces for cyber, operations, third parties, finance, strategy, reputation, and continuity.

Security scan findings flow into the same fix path as other issues, with owners and due dates. Vendor risk sits beside your own.

C · Compliance

Compliance across every obligation you carry

Continuous checks watch controls. Evidence stays linked to what auditors will ask for. When something fails, you open a fix-and-prove loop: correct it, attach proof, then close.

Privacy work, ethics, and packages for external reviewers live in the same system so audit week is preparation, not panic.

    Why I built TigerGRCi.

    I am Sujan Chandra Ray. I work as an ISO/IEC 27001 Lead Auditor and cybersecurity professional. I have run SOC operations and network security in Bangladesh and in Sydney, Australia.

    Across enterprise settings I kept seeing the same gap. National cyber law arrives as text, while GRC tools ship global catalogues and leave local duties to spreadsheets.

    TigerGRCi began as a PMICS research delivery at the University of Dhaka. It is a self-hostable product that treats Bangladesh regulation as first-class work, then connects it to policy, controls, evidence, and fixes with people in the loop.

    The Community exists so practitioners, students, and builders can push the product toward clearer outcomes.
